The True Cost of a Slip-and-Fall in Greater Hartford — and How to Prevent It

Published August 8, 2026 · 4 min read · City Wide Facility Solutions – Greater Hartford

A slip-and-fall accident can happen in seconds — and in Greater Hartford, the risk climbs every time the temperature drops. A dusting of snow tracked through a West Hartford lobby, black ice on a Manchester parking lot, a wet entrance mat after a New England downpour: small hazards that turn into costly claims fast.

The good news is that most slip-and-fall incidents are preventable. With proactive maintenance and regular inspections, Hartford-area businesses can lower risk and keep employees, tenants, and visitors safe through every season.

Slip-and-falls come with a high price tag

Many organizations treat slip-and-falls as isolated incidents. In reality, they carry a major financial impact. According to the Liberty Mutual Workplace Safety Index, falls on the same level — slips, trips, and falls — account for roughly $10.5 billion in direct workers' compensation costs every year, making them one of the most expensive workplace injuries in the country.

Beyond medical costs and comp claims, businesses face lost productivity, overtime, administrative time, and higher insurance premiums.

They're more common than you'd think — especially in a Connecticut winter

Per the National Council on Compensation Insurance, slips, trips, falls, and strains make up more than 60% of lost-time workers' comp claims. In our market, the freeze-thaw cycle from November through March adds ice, slush, and salt residue on top of the year-round hazards found in every office, medical building, school, and warehouse.

Small maintenance problems become bigger safety risks

Most slip-and-falls don't come from one big failure — they come from small issues that go unnoticed. Common hazards around Greater Hartford facilities include:

  • Wet floors from leaks, spills, or tracked-in snow and rain
  • Ice and standing water at entrances, walkways, and parking lots
  • Cracked sidewalks and uneven pavement after a hard freeze
  • Damaged flooring or loose carpet
  • Poor lighting in stairwells and lots that get dark by 4:30 in winter
  • Missing or damaged handrails, and cluttered walkways

Prevention starts with proactive maintenance

The most effective way to reduce risk is to find and fix problems before someone gets hurt. A proactive program for a Connecticut facility should include:

  • Reliable snow and ice management with pre-treatment before storms and clearing at entrances
  • Daytime day porter coverage to catch wet floors, spills, and tracked-in slush during business hours
  • Regular floor care — proper matting, finishes, and non-slip maintenance at entrances
  • Well-stocked, dry restrooms to prevent slick spots
  • Prompt repair of flooring and pavement, and fast response to leaks and spills

Safety is more than compliance

Preventing slip-and-falls isn't just about meeting requirements. It's about protecting the people who use your building every day while avoiding unnecessary cost and disruption. The best facility managers know safety doesn't start after an incident — it's built into daily operations.

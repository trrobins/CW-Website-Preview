Preparing Your Wichita Facility for Kansas Winters: Snow, Ice & Entryway Safety

Kansas winters are unpredictable — a stretch of mild weeks, then a fast-moving ice storm that glazes every parking lot in the metro overnight. For a Wichita facility, winter is less about the total snowfall and more about being ready when it hits. The businesses that stay open and safe are the ones that planned in the fall. Here's how to get your facility winter-ready.

Have a snow and ice plan before the first storm

The worst time to look for a plow is the morning of an ice event, when every contractor in Wichita is already booked. A seasonal snow & ice management agreement sets a plan in advance — clearing triggers, priority areas, and de-icing — so your lot and walks are handled on a schedule instead of a scramble. For any facility that expects employees or customers on-site, that readiness is the whole game.

Entryways are where slip-and-falls happen

Most winter slip-and-fall incidents don't happen in the middle of the parking lot — they happen at the door, where melting snow and tracked-in ice-melt turn a hard floor into a hazard. Walk-off mats, prompt attention to wet entries, and a day porter keeping entrances dry during business hours are the difference between a safe entrance and a liability claim.

Winter is hard on your floors

Salt and ice-melt are brutal on commercial flooring. Tracked across a lobby all winter, they leave a white haze, dull finishes, and damage hard surfaces over time. Stepping up floor care through the winter — more frequent cleaning at entrances, interim scrub-and-recoats — protects both your finish and the safety of the surface.

A quick fall readiness checklist

Before the season turns, confirm a few things: a snow and ice agreement is in place with clear triggers; entrances have adequate walk-off matting; day-porter coverage is set for high-traffic buildings; and your floor-care schedule accounts for winter salt. Handling these in the fall means winter weather is an inconvenience, not a shutdown.

One contact for the whole season

The simplest way to stay ahead of Kansas winter is to have it all under one roof. When your Facility Solutions Manager coordinates snow, entryway coverage, and floor care together, nothing gets missed in the handoff between vendors — and you've got one number to call when the forecast turns.
